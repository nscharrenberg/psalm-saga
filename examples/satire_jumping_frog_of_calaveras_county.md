A narrator visits a dilapidated tavern in a decayed mining camp to ask an old innkeeper about a young minister, Leonidas W. Smiley, whom a friend has asked after. The narrator suspects the minister may not exist. The innkeeper, a talkative and earnest man, takes the question as a chance to tell instead a long story about a different man entirely, Jim Smiley, a compulsive gambler who will bet on anything and who has trained a frog to jump.

The innkeeper's tale is full of digressions: a horse that always loses at the end of races, a bulldog that never lets go, and a frog that wins every contest it enters. It climaxes with a stranger who asks about the frog, then bets against it with a trick.

The narrator tries to get back to the minister, but the innkeeper is interrupted and starts another story, and the narrator leaves without learning anything about the minister.
