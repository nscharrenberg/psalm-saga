# Quotation and Citation — reference

Read this when `adapting-existing-work` identifies the intended relationship as a quotation-bounded excerpt. It mirrors PSALM's Quotation and Citation evaluator (Scharrenberg & Sun, §3.4.3), grounded in Article 5(3)(d) of the InfoSoc Directive and CJEU case law interpreting it, chiefly *Funke Medien* (Case C-469/17, 2019).

This exception is the narrowest of the four defence dimensions: it covers reproducing *specific, identifiable passages* from a source for a recognised purpose (criticism, review, and similar), not stylistic imitation (that's pastiche, see `pastiche.md`) or evocation-through-difference (that's parody, see `parody-and-satire.md`). If the intended piece doesn't actually reproduce identifiable source text, this is probably the wrong exception to work through.

## The Six Sub-Dimensions

### Quotation Identification and Extraction

Which passages, if any, will be reproduced verbatim or near-verbatim, and how they'll be marked (quotation marks, block-quote convention, or another clear signal that this is quoted material rather than the author's own prose). Without actual identifiable quotation, the exception has nothing to attach to — this is a threshold question, not a style choice.

### Legitimate Purpose

The recognised purpose for the quotation: criticism, review, news reporting, teaching, research, or a comparable purpose. State it explicitly rather than leaving the purpose implicit — Article 5(3)(d) is purpose-conditioned, not a general license to excerpt.

### Fair Practice and Proportionality

Whether the extent of quotation is proportionate to that stated purpose, and whether the quoted material is integrated into the author's own original commentary and analysis rather than standing alone with minimal surrounding content. This tracks the "fair practice" language in the Directive and the broader three-step-test tradition in EU copyright law (see Geiger, Gervais, and Senftleben's 2014 treatment of the three-step test's flexibility).

### Attribution and Source Acknowledgement

How the source work and its author will be identified — Article 5(3)(d) requires attribution "unless impossible," so the default expectation is that quotation comes with clear sourcing, not anonymous excerpting.

### Fair Balance and Justification

Whether the quoted material could plausibly substitute for the source in the market, or cause it commercial harm — a background constraint on top of the purpose-and-proportionality analysis above, not a separate license.

### Work Already Disclosed

Confirmation that the source appears to have been lawfully made available to the public already — the quotation exception applies to disclosed works, not unpublished material. This is necessarily limited by what's actually known or verifiable at the time of writing; note explicitly if this is uncertain rather than assuming it.

## Further Reading

- European Parliament and Council, Directive 2001/29/EC (InfoSoc), Art. 5(3)(d) — the statutory basis for the quotation exception.
- Court of Justice of the European Union, *Funke Medien* (Case C-469/17, 2019) — CJEU interpretation of quotation-adjacent exceptions and their limits.
- Geiger, C., Gervais, D., and Senftleben, M. (2014), "The three-step test revisited: How to use the test's flexibility in national copyright law," *American University International Law Review* 29(3) — background on the proportionality reasoning that underlies the "fair practice" requirement above.
